1. Algebraic Expressions

Use a letter to stand for a number. Read the words carefully:

  • “5 is added to x” → x + 5
  • “Three times a number, decreased by 7” → 3a − 7
  • “Half of n, increased by 8” → n/2 + 8
  • “The difference between twice y and 9” → 2y − 9
Example: If x = 6, then 4x + 3 = 24 + 3 = 27. If a = 4 and b = 3, then 2a + 5b = 8 + 15 = 23.

2. Writing Equations from Words

Turn the story into an equation, then solve it.

Example: “A student has m marbles. His friend has 4 more marbles. Together they have 20.” → m + (m + 4) = 20 → 2m + 4 = 20 → m = 8.

Always check your answer by substitution: 8 + 12 = 20 ✓

3. Three-Dimensional Shapes

  • Cube: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices. Its net is six equal squares.
  • Cuboid: V = length × width × height. Example: 8 × 5 × 3 = 120 cm³.
  • Cylinder: two circular faces and one curved surface.
  • Sphere: one curved surface, no edges, no vertices.
  • Square-based pyramid: 5 faces, 8 edges, 5 vertices.
  • Triangular prism: 5 faces, 9 edges, 6 vertices.
Euler's formula: V − E + F = 2
Example: A solid has 8 vertices and 12 edges → 8 − 12 + F = 2 → F = 6.

4. Factors, Multiples, HCF & LCM

  • Factor: divides exactly. 9 is a factor of 72 (72 ÷ 9 = 8).
  • Multiple: 24 is a multiple of both 6 and 8.
  • Prime number: only divisible by 1 and itself. 31 is prime.
  • Prime factorisation: 60 = 2² × 3 × 5.
  • HCF: largest common factor. HCF of 36 and 48 = 12.
  • LCM: smallest common multiple. LCM of 12 and 18 = 36.
Example: Bells ring every 8 and 12 minutes → LCM = 24 → next together at 08.24. 48 pencils and 60 erasers → HCF = 12 packages.
